---
title: Why is sitting so bad for you?
date: 2026-11-14
topic: Exercise
---
**Sitting switches off your largest muscles.**

When these muscles stop contracting for prolonged periods, insulin sensitivity drops, blood vessel health declines, and the enzymes that break down fat slow down. Over time, this leads to high blood sugar, high blood pressure, and weight gain.

A [2022 study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12603-022-1727-6) tracked more than 17,000 adults in rural China for 6 years. Compared to sitting less than 4 hours a day:

- Sitting 8–11 hours a day was linked to a **27% higher risk of death**.
- Sitting 11+ hours a day was linked to a **48% higher risk of death**.

Each extra hour of daily sitting was linked to a 3% higher risk of death.

You can offset this risk by moving more throughout the day. Start with small changes to your current routine:

- Use a standing desk if you work at a computer.
- Take phone calls and meetings while walking.
- Set a reminder to stand every 30 minutes.
- Take the stairs instead of the elevator.
- Stretch while watching TV.
- Park farther away.

If you can’t sit less, [research suggests](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2816%2930370-1) 60–75 minutes a day of moderate exercise, like brisk walking, can offset most of the risk of prolonged sitting.

**When in doubt, choose movement.**
