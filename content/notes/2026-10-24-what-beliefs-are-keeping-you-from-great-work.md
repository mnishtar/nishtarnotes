---
title: What beliefs are keeping you from Great Work?
date: 2026-10-24
---
**Great Work is a practice of faith.**

You have faith that creating something different today will one day have a positive influence on yourself, others and the world.

To sustain that faith, you first have to reverse these limiting beliefs:

### 1. Freedom vs. Constraints

**Belief:** *More freedom makes me more productive.*

**Truth:** Unrestricted freedom leads to inefficiency. Constraints reduce your freedom, but they force you to be creative, and creativity makes you more productive.

### 2. Busyness vs. Productivity

**Belief:** *If I’m busy, I’m being productive.*

**Truth:** Busy does not mean better. When you fill your day with activity, you dilute your focus and make little progress on the work that matters most.

### 3. Credentials vs. Courage

**Belief:** *I need the right degree before I start.*

**Truth:** You don’t need a degree for Great Work. You need the courage to ask and the willingness to solve problems for others.

### 4. Motivation vs. Environment

**Belief:** *I’ll start when I feel motivated.*

**Truth:** Motivation doesn’t last. The spaces where you live and work shape most of your daily habits.

### 5. Winning vs. Improving

**Belief:** *Fast results matter most.*

**Truth:** Fast results often come at the expense of long-term improvement. Focus on getting better, and the wins will follow.

### 6. Intensity vs. Consistency

**Belief:** *The harder I push, the better.*

**Truth:** Intensity you can’t sustain leads to burnout. Build the skill of showing up at a pace you can keep for years.

### 7. News vs. Old Books

**Belief:** *Watching the news keeps me informed.*

**Truth:** Most news is forgotten by tomorrow. Great books that are still read after 10 years have lasted because their ideas are timeless.

### 8. Fear of Judgment vs. Sharing

**Belief:** *It’s safer to keep my work to myself.*

**Truth:** Fear keeps your work hidden. When you share your unique ideas with the world, you inspire others to pursue their own Great Work.
