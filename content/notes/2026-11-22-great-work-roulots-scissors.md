In 2001, the French artisan Jean-Marie Roulot donated more than 4,000 items to the city of Nogent, France.

It was a vintage collection of scissors, cutlery, machinery, and blacksmithing tools. Foreign buyers had made lucrative offers for it, but Roulot wanted to preserve local heritage.

One of the most famous items in his collection was a pair of golden scissors.

Roulot was a master *ciselier* (scissor-maker) in Nogent. At 14, he apprenticed in a scissor factory, the beginning of a lifetime spent crafting embroidery scissors by hand. When he finished a pair, he listened for the blades to “sing,” a sign the scissors were balanced and perfected.

His creations were as useful as they were beautiful. Consider, for example, his stork scissors:

- The beak is where the two blades meet.
- The eye is the nail that holds them together.
- The gold-plated body is the base.
- The feet are the loops your fingers slip through.

**Roulot made the mundane beautiful.**

That is Great Work. It means caring about the little things, pouring effort and beauty into everyday tasks and objects.

**Beauty ennobles the ordinary.**
