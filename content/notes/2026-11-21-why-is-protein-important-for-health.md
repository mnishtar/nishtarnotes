---
title: Why is protein important for health?
date: 2026-11-21
topic: Diet
---
Protein is one of three essential macronutrients and part of every organ in your body. Choosing the right amount and source is key to any evidence-based health protocol.

## How much?

Aim for [1.2 to 1.6 grams](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26960445/) of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, and a little more if you do resistance training.

**Higher protein is linked to a longer life.** It helps you build and keep muscle, which is linked to longevity, and it’s tied to modestly better blood pressure and blood fats. One [analysis of 31 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32699048/) found that people who ate the most protein had a 6% lower risk of death from any cause.

It also makes weight loss easier. Protein lowers hunger hormones after a meal and helps you keep lean muscle as the weight comes off.

## From where?

**Prioritize plant protein.** In that same analysis, people who ate the most plant protein had an 8% lower risk of death. Each extra 3% of daily calories from plant protein was tied to a 5% lower risk.

A higher plant-to-animal ratio is also linked to a healthier heart. A [30-year Harvard study](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/higher-ratio-of-plant-protein-to-animal-protein-may-improve-heart-health) found that people with the highest ratio had a 19% lower risk of cardiovascular disease and a 27% lower risk of coronary artery disease. Most of the benefit likely came from replacing red and processed meat.

So minimize deli meats, fatty beef, and hot dogs. Instead, try the protein sources in Dr. Michael Greger’s [Daily Dozen](https://nutritionfacts.org/daily-dozen/):

- Legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans)
- Nuts and seeds
- Whole grains

If you eat meat, swap some animal protein for plant protein to tip the plant-to-animal ratio in your favor.

**How much protein you eat matters. Where it comes from matters more.**
