In 1894, the Danish painter Peder Mørk Mønsted gave himself an impossible task. He wanted to paint sunlight in a forest.

The problem is that forest light never holds still. It lands on one leaf and skips the next. It shines on water until a cloud passes by. To paint a scene like this, Mønsted had to understand how every piece of light behaved, then set each one down by hand, in oil.

The result is *Wooded path by a river*.

He didn’t paint a mountain or a cathedral. He painted a dirt path by the water, the kind most of us walk past every day without a second thought. The beauty was there for anyone to see. Mønsted was simply the one who stopped long enough to notice it.

**Great Work starts with seeing what everyone else walks past.**
