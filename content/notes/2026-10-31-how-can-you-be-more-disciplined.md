---
title: How can you be more disciplined?
date: 2026-10-31
---
There’s a simple formula that explains how you can become more disciplined.

Once you learn it, you can use it on any goal you set.

**Discipline = Motivation > Resistance**

Put another way, you are disciplined when your motivation is greater than your resistance.

Two things create your motivation:

- **Goal (G):** the specific result you want
- **Risk (R):** what you put on the line so acting now feels urgent

Two things create your resistance:

- **Inertia (I):** how hard the task feels before you start
- **Pull (P):** the distractions pulling your attention

Put it together and you get GRIP:

**Discipline = Goal × Risk > Inertia + Pull**

## GRIP in action: waking up early

Say you want to wake up early. Here’s how you’d run the formula:

- **Goal:** Wake up by 4 AM.
- **Risk:** If you don’t wake up on time, you don’t get to write (any activity you enjoy or wish to prioritize).
- **Inertia:** Set your alarm away from your bed.
- **Pull:** Block all phone apps until 6 AM.

As long as Goal × Risk is greater than Inertia + Pull, you’ll wake up on time.

## Run it on your next goal

Next time you set a goal, run the equation:

1. **Set a Big Goal (G).** Put a number on it. “Wake up early” is vague. “Up by 4 AM” is something you can measure.
2. **Define your Risk (R).** This is the most powerful lever. A result a year away means little to your brain today. Real consequences make the future matter now.
3. **Minimize Inertia (I).** This is the next most powerful lever. Shrink the first step to one two-minute action that takes no thought.
4. **Reduce Pull (P).** Put your phone, or your food, out of easy reach.

Then check: is Goal × Risk bigger than Inertia + Pull?

If yes, you’ll do it.

If no, raise your Risk or shrink your Inertia.

You don’t get results by setting better goals.

**You get results when not doing the thing costs more than doing it.**
