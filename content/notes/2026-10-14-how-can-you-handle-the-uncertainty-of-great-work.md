---
title: How can you handle the uncertainty of Great Work?
date: 2026-10-14
---
You may never get to enjoy the fruits of your labor.

The art you make may inspire a generation you never meet. The child you raise may never understand your love. The work you do may prevent a disaster that no one knows to thank you for.

The work itself is the reward.

*…Maybe.*

That “maybe” is fear talking. Your mind questions your every move because it believes one wrong decision can decide your well-being. And so your thoughts race with uncertainty, trying to find a way to make you feel okay.

But here’s the truth:

You’re asking a mind full of fear how to be at peace. But your mind is causing the problem in the first place.

Notice how it pulls you in, making everything feel urgent and important:

- *Should I work on this art project or this writing project?*
- *Should my kids do more activities, or fewer?*
- *Should I share this work, or keep it to myself?*

The more you engage with the mind, the louder it gets.

So don’t try to quiet your mind. Step back and observe it.

When you see the mind jump from thought to thought, you stop taking it so seriously. You stop believing its doubts hold the key to your peace. You don’t have to fix the mind or answer every thought. You only have to notice one thing:

**If you can watch the mind, you are not the mind.**

That is where freedom starts.

From there, you trust life to guide you. Decisions unfold without struggle. You let go of the false belief that your peace depends on getting what the mind wants.

That is how you make room for Great Work.

Observe the mind, ignore its distractions, and trust life to take care of itself.
