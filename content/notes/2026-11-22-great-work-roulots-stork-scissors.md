In 2001, the French artisan Jean-Marie Roulot donated more than 4,000 items to the city of Nogent, France.

It was a vintage collection of scissors, cutlery, machinery, and blacksmithing tools, gathered over many years. Foreign buyers had made lucrative offers for it, but Roulot wanted to preserve local heritage.

Roulot was a master *ciselier* (scissor-maker) in Nogent. At 14, he apprenticed in a scissor factory, the start of a lifetime spent making embroidery scissors by hand. When he finished a pair, he listened for the blades to “sing.” A clean song meant they were balanced and true.

His scissors were as useful as they were beautiful. Consider, for example, his stork scissors:

- The beak is the blades.
- The eye is the nail that holds them together.
- The gold-plated body is the base.
- The feet are the loops your fingers slip through.

**Roulot made the mundane beautiful.**

That is Great Work. It means caring about the little things, pouring effort and beauty into everyday tasks and objects.

**Beauty ennobles the ordinary.**
