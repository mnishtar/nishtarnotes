Tribes is built on one idea from behavioral science. Habits stick better when you build them with other people.

66% of people who joined a weight-loss program with friends or family, and practiced supporting each other, kept their full weight loss six months after the program ended.
24% of people who joined alone did the same.

Wing RR, Jeffery RW. Benefits of recruiting participants with friends and increasing social support for weight loss and maintenance. Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology, 1999;67(1):132–138.

How it works

Three steps, about two minutes to set up.

  1. 1

    Start your tribe

    Create a tribe, give it a name and invite your family. Only people you invite can join.

  2. 2

    Set one shared goal

    Write it as a plan: when it happens, and what you'll do together. A specific plan beats a vague wish.

  3. 3

    Check in each day

    Log your day in seconds and see everyone's progress. A daily reminder nudges anyone who forgets, so nobody has to nag.

Five habits. One place.

Tribes is built around the five pillars I write about every week.

  • MeditationA few quiet minutes, every day.
  • ExerciseSteps, workouts, movement of any kind.
  • DietMeals that give you energy instead of taking it.
  • SleepThe habit that powers all the others.
  • Great WorkTime on the work that matters most to you.

Built for families, not competition.

Most fitness apps rank you against strangers. Tribes shows your family's progress side by side and keeps one shared streak. Nobody wins by making someone else lose. You keep each other going.

Why I built it.

As a pain physician, I see what years of small daily choices add up to. Medicine is good at treating the damage and far less good at helping people prevent it. So I built the tool I wanted for my own family. We're training to climb a mountain together in March, and Tribes is how we keep each other moving.
